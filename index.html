AI enablement, explained simply

My job: take a company from "a few people play with ChatGPT" to "everyone uses AI every day, safely, and it shows in the numbers". Here is the whole journey, end to end:

  1. Listen first I spend the first days watching how your teams really work — where time is lost, what is repetitive, what people fear about AI.
  2. Find the champions In every company, a few people are already getting real value from AI. I find them and make them the visible example others want to follow.
  3. Set simple safety rules Which data may go into which tool — written in plain words everyone remembers. Not a 50-page policy nobody opens.
  4. Pick quick wins Two or three low-risk, highly visible use cases, so the whole company sees a benefit within weeks — like the recurring analysis at Crédit Agricole that got 60% faster.
  5. Train hands-on No slides, no theory theatre. Everyone practises on their own real work — beginners get confidence, advanced users become power users.
  6. Measure what matters Not attendance sheets: adoption (who actually uses it weekly) and one business number, agreed up front.
Why "hands-on" is the whole secret. You cannot teach someone to swim with a slideshow — and you cannot teach AI with one either. Confidence only comes from doing: typing the prompt yourself, seeing it fail, fixing it, seeing it win on your own work. That's when the habit sticks. And I hold myself to the same rule: I build and run my own AI systems every day — my own cloud, my own agents, this very website. I never recommend anything I haven't done with my own hands. People follow a guide who walks the path, not one who points at the map.
